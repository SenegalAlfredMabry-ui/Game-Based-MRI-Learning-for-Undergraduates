Part A, Step 3 - Open, Review, and Compare Images in ITK-SNAP
=============================================================

Goal
----

Open one subject's FLAIR and MPRAGE images in ITK-SNAP, review each scan by
itself, and then compare them before drawing any lesion masks.

By the end of this part, you should be able to say:

    I know where my converted NIfTI files are.
    I can open a FLAIR image in ITK-SNAP.
    I can open or compare a MPRAGE image in ITK-SNAP.
    I can explain why FLAIR and MPRAGE show different information.


Step 1 - Confirm the converted files exist
------------------------------------------

Use what you already practiced in the House, Wine, Laundry, and Scan Converter
games. Start by checking where you are and listing the folders.

You are looking for files like:

    converted_niftis/sub-PD01/sub-PD01_FLAIR.nii.gz
    converted_niftis/sub-PD01/sub-PD01_MPRAGE.nii.gz

Pause question:
    Which command tells you where you are before you start opening files?


Step 2 - Open ITK-SNAP
----------------------

Open ITK-SNAP from your Applications folder, Launchpad, Start menu, or the
normal app menu on your computer.

If ITK-SNAP will not open, stop and read:

    cat SOFTWARE_SETUP.txt

Do not skip this problem. If the viewer does not open, the marking step will
not work.


Step 3 - Review the FLAIR first
-------------------------------

Load the FLAIR as the main image.

For the first practice subject, use:

    converted_niftis/sub-PD01/sub-PD01_FLAIR.nii.gz

In ITK-SNAP, move through the slices slowly. FLAIR is especially useful because
fluid signal is suppressed and many lesion-like white matter hyperintensities
can appear brighter than the surrounding tissue.

Adjust the display/contrast if the FLAIR looks too dark, washed out, or flat.
You are trying to make bright lesion-like regions visible without turning the
whole brain bright.

Pause question:
    What looks bright on the FLAIR image, and what looks dark?


Step 4 - Review the MPRAGE separately
-------------------------------------

Now review the MPRAGE for the same subject.

Use:

    converted_niftis/sub-PD01/sub-PD01_MPRAGE.nii.gz

MPRAGE gives a strong structural/anatomical view. It is useful for checking
where you are in the brain and whether a bright area on FLAIR makes anatomical
sense.

Pause question:
    What does the MPRAGE make easier to see than the FLAIR?


Step 5 - Compare FLAIR and MPRAGE
---------------------------------

If your ITK-SNAP version lets you add another image, load FLAIR as the main
image and add MPRAGE as an additional image or overlay. If your version does
not make that easy, open the two images one at a time and compare the same
slice location carefully.

You can also run this helper to print the exact files for a subject:

    bash scripts/open_subject_in_itksnap_TEMPLATE.sh sub-PD01

If your computer has an ITK-SNAP command-line launcher, the helper may open the
images. If not, it will still tell you which files to load manually.

Pause question:
    Why is it useful to compare the FLAIR to the MPRAGE before drawing a mask?


Step 6 - Write a short checkpoint
---------------------------------

Open:

    nano student_notes/part3_itksnap_reflection.txt

Answer the checkpoint questions before starting Part B.


Part A stop point
-----------------

Before moving on, confirm that you can:

1. Find the converted NIfTI files.
2. Open FLAIR in ITK-SNAP.
3. Open or compare MPRAGE in ITK-SNAP.
4. Adjust the FLAIR display so bright regions are visible.
5. Explain what FLAIR and MPRAGE each help you inspect.
